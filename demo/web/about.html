How it was made

A large model wrote the example answers, and a small open model learned from them. Only the small model runs here.

  1. 📝SentencesEveryday sentences in English, French and Spanish: half real ones from Tatoeba, half generated by an AI model.
  2. 🧑‍🏫Teacher labelsDeepSeek V4.1 Flash turns each sentence into emojis, following written rules and examples chosen by the author.
  3. 🏋️Fine-tuningA LoRA adapter is trained on Qwen3.5 2B, a small open model, with 5,400 of these pairs on a Colab T4 GPU in about 25 minutes.
  4. 🧪ScoringA reader model guesses the sentence back from the emojis, and a judge compares the guess with the original.
  5. 🖥️ServingThe model runs in 8-bit with llama.cpp on a small CPU server, loads when someone visits and unloads when idle.

How good is it?

The score is a round trip: Claude Haiku reads only the emojis and guesses the sentence, then Claude Sonnet checks whether the guess keeps the topic, the event and the outcome. 1 means the gist came back whole.

ModelWhat it isScore
v1Qwen3.5 2B fine-tuned on 3,000 English and French sentences0.47
v2 (this demo)Qwen3.5 2B fine-tuned on 5,400 sentences in three languages0.47
v2, 4BThe same training on Qwen3.5 4B, twice the size0.52
TeacherDeepSeek V4.1 Flash with the full rules and examples0.57

300 development sentences, 100 per language. The 4B gain is real (+0.05, 95% interval +0.01 to +0.08) but small for a model twice as heavy and slow, so the demo keeps the 2B.

The round-trip score sees v1 and v2 as equal. In a blind side-by-side review of 100 new sentences, Sonnet preferred v2 more often than v1 (net +0.14, not proven): cleaner answers that show who acts and get times right, while v1 keeps more small details.

In the first version, fine-tuning raised the score of the untrained model from 0.81 to 1.13 on a 0–2 scale, closing 64% of the gap to the teacher. Tick Compare on the demo page to see the untrained model next to v1 and v2.

Limits

  • Idioms and abstract ideas are hard: “llover a cántaros” (to rain buckets) once came back with a glass of milk 🥛.
  • Long sentences lose details, and numbers or times can come out wrong.
  • The model follows its reading conventions most of the time, not always.
  • It's a small model: it can get things wrong, sometimes confidently.

Privacy

Sentences are not logged or stored. A share link keeps only the sentence, after the # in the address, a part that browsers never send to the server. Opening it runs the model again, which always gives the same answer to the same sentence.

AI disclosure

  • Training labels and part of the training sentences come from DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. Real sentences come from Tatoeba (CC BY 2.0 FR).
  • Claude is used only as an evaluator (reader and judge), never to write, select or filter training data.
  • Claude also helped write the code, the documentation and this page's text, and drafted the example sentences. None of them is used for training.
